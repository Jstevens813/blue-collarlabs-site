The Gear We Actually Run in the Shop

People ask us all the time what 3D printer and filament we use to make the desk toys, launchers and keychains in our Etsy shop. This page is the honest answer: the exact machines and materials on our own bench in St. Petersburg, Florida, with notes on what each one is good for and who should buy it. Nothing here is listed because a brand paid us to list it — if a spool or a machine did not earn its place in a working print shop that ships real products every week, it is not on this page. Start with the printer, then match your filament to the job using the guide below.

The printer — Bambu Lab P1S

This is the machine behind every Blue-Collar Labs product. The Bambu Lab P1S is fast, enclosed, and reliable enough to run production parts all day — the enclosed chamber keeps temperatures stable for cleaner prints, and the speed means a desk toy that took all afternoon on an older printer finishes in a fraction of the time. If you are buying your first 3D printer for a home workshop, this is the one we recommend without hesitation: it prints beautifully right out of the box and grows with you into tougher materials later. The Combo version adds the AMS unit for multi-color prints and automatic filament switching — worth it if you plan on multi-color designs like our his-and-hers keychains, or if you just hate swapping spools by hand.

The filament — Bambu Lab PLA Basic

Our everyday filament for desk toys and prototypes. PLA is the easiest filament to print — low warping, no special ventilation needs, and clean detail — which makes it the right first spool for any beginner. Bambu's PLA Basic adds consistent diameter, reliable prints, and an RFID spool that works with the AMS so the printer knows exactly what is loaded and picks the right settings by itself. If you are brand new to 3D printing, buy this first and learn the machine before you chase exotic materials.

PLA+ — the tougher everyday filament

PLA+ prints as easy as regular PLA but with noticeably better impact strength and layer adhesion — our pick when a part needs to take a knock, not just sit on a desk. Think keychains, tool holders, brackets and anything a kid or a coworker will get their hands on. eSUN's PLA+ is the shop standard here: consistent spool after spool, and the price per kilo is hard to beat for the quality. If you only stock one filament beyond basic PLA, make it PLA+.

PETG & PETG-CF — functional parts

When a print has to live outside, survive heat in a truck cab, or hold real weight, we reach for PETG. PETG HF is our go-to for durable, weather-resistant parts that print nearly as fast as PLA, with much better heat and impact resistance. When a part needs real stiffness, PETG-CF adds carbon fiber for strength and a matte finish that hides layer lines — just run it with a hardened steel nozzle, because carbon fiber eats brass nozzles alive. Bambu sells PETG-CF direct, so that link goes to their store instead of Amazon.

It is our choice for display pieces, gifts and anything that gets photographed, because the matte finish reads as a finished product instead of a prototype. Great for display pieces and gifts.

PLA vs PLA+ vs PETG — Which Filament Should You Buy?

Here is the short version we give every beginner. PLA is for learning, prototypes and display pieces: easiest to print, best detail, but it softens in a hot car. PLA+ is the everyday workhorse: nearly as easy to print, meaningfully tougher, and the right default for parts that get used and handled. PETG is for functional parts that face heat, weather or stress: a little more finicky to dial in, but far more durable outdoors and under load. PETG-CF is the stiff, premium option for parts that must not flex — with the hardened-nozzle warning above. Buy in that order as your projects grow, and you will never have a spool sitting around that you cannot use.

Do I need the AMS?

Only if you want multi-color prints or hands-off filament switching. Every filament on this page prints fine on a standalone P1S; the AMS is a convenience and a creative upgrade, not a requirement.
